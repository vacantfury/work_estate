# Project pipeline — sourcing to landing

*(Renamed from `project-lifecycle.md` at v6.0.0: this module is the PIPELINE — which projects exist and how they land; the per-project scaffold and record live in `project-home.md`, the build funnel in `dev-workflow.md`.)*

Projects are the unit of career-visible work: **what is worked on determines delivered impact more than how well it is executed.** So the whole project arc is a standard workflow, run continuously as a **pipeline** — many candidates tracked, few active — never a one-shot reaction at the moment an assignment arrives. **Provisional by provenance** (axis 2 of the build model, `text_docs/design.md`): the lifecycle conforms to the team's real project process (design principle 10) — every stage rides the team's own ceremonies (planning, staffing, demo slots, review rhythm) wherever they exist; this module fills gaps and governs personal discipline only.

## The portfolio board — standing state

- One work-local board (the unfold seeds `local/state/projects.md`) holds every candidate and active project, one line each: `name · stage · sponsor · why-it-matters · next action · next-show` (next-show = the date/venue where it next becomes visible: demo, readout, sync). Stage names come from this module.
- Reviewed on a fixed cadence aligned with the team's planning rhythm and the review cycle. The portfolio questions: is the mix right (reliable delivery + at least one growth bet)? is concurrency honest? will each active project's impact **land before the next review checkpoint** — impact that lands after a review cannot be counted at it?
- A zombie entry — no stage movement, no next action — is a defect: revive it, exit it, or drop it consciously.

## Stage 1 — sourcing (continuous)

- Collect candidates on sight, evaluation deferred: team backlog and charter items, leadership asks, pain points nobody owns, adjacent teams' needs, own ideas — and rising themes from the company watch (`company-watch.md`), the pipeline's forward-looking source. The entry bar is one board line.
- **Selection power is earned, not given.** Early tenure runs assigned work reliably while sourcing and intelligence run in the background; the right to choose — and to refuse — grows with delivered standing.

## Stage 2 — intelligence (per candidate worth a look)

- Active gathering before deciding — the docs, the artifacts, AND going to ask people directly (the principle-10 triple source applied to projects): who sponsors it and how far up the care extends · why now · what's been tried before and why it died · what success would look like and how it would be measured · who else wants it · the effort shape and the dependencies.
- Asking people is a first-class intelligence move, not an imposition — and each such conversation doubles as relationship investment. Record what's known vs unknown, so the decision names its own uncertainty.

## Stage 3 — evaluate and decide

The criteria pass (the checklist is standard; the weights are judgment):

- **Alignment** — on the critical path of what the org visibly values (the charter, leadership attention, what recent promotions were built on)? Judged against the LIVE direction picture — the company watch's theme register (`company-watch.md`), never a stale snapshot; the watch's planning-rhythm refresh re-scores this column across the whole board.
- **Legibility** — does success produce something showable: a metric move, a demo, a readout people actually read? Illegible work is real work that doesn't accumulate.
- **Ownership** — the person on it, or one of five?
- **Growth** — does it produce evidence of next-level scope?
- **Dead-end signals** — no sponsor above the manager · rescue/maintenance with no recognition path · success undefined or unmeasurable · blocked on dependencies nobody controls.
- **Cost** — expected total time-and-effort spend against the impact (the principle-9 target applies to selection first: the cheapest effort is the project not taken).
- **People** — who it would be worked with; the allies it would build.

Decision outcomes, each with its stage-4 communication: **accept** · **shape** (counter-scope: a smaller version, a time-boxed spike, "yes after X ships") · **decline via priorities** · **chase** · **initiate** (own proposal → stage 5). An **accept** also runs the project premortem (`risk-register.md`): likely failure modes with early signals and pre-planned reactions, declared before work starts — the twin of the stage-8 landing declaration. And an accept FOUNDS the project's home under main-work — the project-founding procedure (`portable-skills.md`) copies the shipped scaffold (`project-home.md`): task surfaces, the record, registry row with edges, board line.

## Stage 4 — communication and negotiation

- **Refusal is never a bare no.** Route it through prioritization: "I can take this, or keep X on schedule — which should win?" puts the trade honestly in the hands that own it. Capacity and fit framing only — never argue the project is worthless, never badmouth work being avoided.
- **Shaping beats declining.** Decline a version, never the mission; keep the door open for the right version.
- **Chasing happens before staffing.** Interest expressed early and lightly to the people who staff projects; small adjacent tasks volunteered for a foothold; the relevant capability made visible — so being picked is the natural outcome. Interest declared at staffing time is too late.
- **The manager is the standing channel.** Project intentions, capacity limits, and portfolio shape stay aligned in the regular 1:1 — the review conversation should hold no surprises about what was and wasn't taken on.

## Stage 5 — initiative lane (self-originated projects)

- Own ideas earn their pitch with evidence: a time-boxed, low-cost PoC or spike **first**, then the proposal — problem → evidence (the PoC result, data) → proposed scope → cost → ask. Proposals go through the team's own channel for new work (discovered at intake), never around it.
- Calibrate initiative to standing: early tenure proposes small, adjacent, and low-risk; scale with credibility. An accepted initiative becomes a normal active project — same board, same cadence, same landing bar.

## Stage 6 — execution (daily dev under the project frame)

- The build itself runs the staged development funnel (`dev-workflow.md`): whole-product design → substructure design naming the consequential decision points → choice tournaments settled by small pre-experiments (the clearly-bad excluded, survivors advance) → a few comparable variants → the environment ladder (local test → shared test environment → production for the surviving one or two, monitored at every rung) → completion declared at the stage-8 landing bar.
- Daily work runs per the playbook's loops (`role-playbook.md`). The project frame adds: reviewable increments at the pace the human review loop absorbs, visible momentum, and the project's running story kept current — status honest and early, especially on slips.

## Stage 7 — sync and presentation (periodic, per active project)

- Every active project carries a declared sync cadence and a next-show date; both live on the board. Syncs ride the team's existing ceremonies (sprint readouts, demo slots, 1:1s) rather than inventing parallel ones.
- **Presentation is part of the work, not after it:** demos at natural milestones, readouts in the channels leadership actually reads, and the shared delivered-work record updated as things land. Readouts speak the current direction vocabulary — the company watch's reframe rung (`company-watch.md`) applied to presentation.

## Stage 8 — landing (the defined end)

- Every accepted project declares its end at accept time: what shipped + measured + presented + credited looks like. **Work is done when the impact is legible to the people it matters to** — readout written, demo given, record updated — not when the code merges.
- Three end states, all explicit: **landed** · **handed off** (managed: documented state, a named receiver, a clean cut) · **killed** (a conscious call with a short readout — killing deliberately is respectable; drifting dead is not). Extension is a conscious re-bet, never a silent lapse.
- Exits are remembered as much as launches: a graceful handoff builds the same reputation a good landing does.
