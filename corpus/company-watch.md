# Company watch — leadership, people, and direction intelligence

What the company's leadership and high-level people are doing is the highest-value intelligence stream the job offers: what they formally lead and visibly push, what they say casually, who is promoted and for what, who departs — exited or laid off — and for what, reorgs and funding moves, and the direction it all adds up to. This module is the standing system that watches it: signals collected continuously from surfaces already attended, graded by evidence strength, distilled into longitudinal registers, and consumed by three things — the project pipeline's **alignment** criterion (`project-lifecycle.md`, stage 3), **behavior calibration** (the reward map: what the company actually rewards and punishes), and **relationship aiming** (people trajectory). **The watch is an intelligence organ, never a decision channel** — everything it recommends flows through the lifecycle machinery and the working conventions.

**Its field is the whole company.** The own reporting chain is watched most closely — it decides daily work — but people and events outside the org are never out of scope: **proximity weights attention; it never draws the boundary.**

Its compounding property is longitudinal: a snapshot can say what leadership says; only a kept record can say what is *rising* — themes and people both. The registers are built to be kept for years.

## The direction model — three levels, one company-wide field

- **Company** — top leadership: company-wide memos, public statements, flagship bets.
- **Org** — the leadership chain the team reports through: division priorities, planning artifacts, reorgs, staffing moves.
- **Team** — the manager and the charter: what the team is actually being asked to carry.

Alignment is a **chain**: the work → the team charter → org priorities → company direction. Each level moves at its own rate and has its own surfaces. A break in the chain — the team's direction diverging from the org's — is itself a first-class finding: it prices the risk of everything on the team's path and is worth knowing early.

The chain is the alignment spine, **not the collection boundary**: adjacent orgs' moves, other divisions' leaders, and company-wide personnel events are fully in scope. Cross-org signals matter doubly — they often show company direction earlier than the local chain does, and they mark the people worth knowing beyond the silo.

## The evidence ladder — signals graded by durability

Ordered strongest to weakest; a signal's grade decides what it may justify:

1. **Resourcing** — headcount moved, budget granted, a reorg executed, an executive hired to own it. Organizations state their real priorities with money and people; everything else is provisional.
2. **Repetition** — the same theme sustained across leadership levels and across quarters/planning cycles.
3. **Planning artifacts** — OKRs, commitments, charters, roadmap entries.
4. **Reward evidence** — what recent promotions, awards, and visibly-credited wins were actually built on.
5. **Rhetoric** — a keynote line, a one-off all-hands mention, a hallway remark. Logged and watched, never chased.

Personnel events sit naturally at the top grades: a promotion is reward evidence about behavior AND resourcing evidence for its theme; a layoff is resourcing evidence in the negative. The module's named failure mode is the **weathervane**: pivoting on every announcement delivers nothing, burns credibility, and reads as unseriousness. Only resourcing-backed or sustained-repeated direction justifies moving work; lower grades feed the record until they earn a higher one.

## The store — one log, four registers (`local/state/company.md`)

Unfold-seeded, work-local:

- **Signal log** (append-only, the ground layer): `date · source (who + venue, level) · signal · grade`. One line per signal, captured at the moment of exposure — formal announcements and casual remarks alike. The running cost is minutes per week.
- **Leader register:** one entry per person — `person · role/level · formally leads · visibly pushes · notable casual signals · trajectory (rising / steady / falling) · last confirmed`. Scope: the reporting chain up through company leadership, the org's visibly influential people, and notable leaders elsewhere in the company — proximity weights attention, never filters entry.
- **Event register:** dated events, the factual record — promotions (who, for what) · departures, exits, layoffs (what, why as best known) · reorgs, funding and staffing moves, org-local and company-wide alike. Ground truth the derived views distill from.
- **Theme register** (derived view): one entry per direction theme — `theme · level · lifecycle state (emerging / rising / established / fading) · strongest evidence grade · last confirmed · portfolio implication`.
- **Reward map** (derived view): what this company actually promotes, tolerates, and penalizes, distilled from the event register across cycles. It calibrates own behavior and project selection more sharply than any stated value does.

Updates are new dated log entries first, registers second; history is never rewritten, because trajectory — of themes and of people — is read from it. The schema is deliberately typed so the record stays machine-readable: markdown is the default medium, and if the sanctioned work tooling supports something more structured, the same schema carries over — the unfold's call.

## The record's posture — candid and complete

The store lives on the work device, and records **candidly and completely, for the user's benefit** (settled 2026-08-12): observations, trajectory reads, honest analysis of events — what actually happened and what it means. The content is knowledge legitimately gained through ordinary work and violates no obligation; completeness is never traded away for appearance. The user still keeps their own career view beyond the job — not out of caution, but because it outlives any single job: person relationships are lifelong assets, and what company events mean for the career is a beyond-the-job question; debriefs travel via the boundary discipline (`boundary-protocol.md`).

## Collection — riding existing surfaces

Never a new ceremony. Capture happens at moments of exposure already lived: all-hands and town halls at each level · leadership memos and company/org announcements · personnel announcements — promotion lists, departure notes, org-chart changes, in the own org and beyond it · planning cycles, where OKRs and commitments get set · promotion and calibration language · casual remarks in meetings and hallways (logged like formal signals, graded as rhetoric until repeated) · cross-org channels and other divisions' visible moves · and the **manager 1:1, the standing direct channel** — "what is leadership pushing this half, and what is getting headcount" is a normal question, not an imposition, and doubles as relationship investment.

The **public lane** — executive moves, layoff and reorg press coverage, earnings themes, leadership statements outside the company — is the user's own concern off this machine, watched by whatever public-information feeds they keep; what matters reaches this installation in their own word. The work side runs no external feed tooling.

## Distillation — cadence and coupling

Refresh the registers on the team's planning rhythm — the natural checkpoint before each portfolio review — plus event-triggered on a reorg, a leadership change, a promotion cycle, or a major announcement. A refresh re-reads the recent log, updates trajectories and lifecycle states, re-dates confirmations, refreshes the reward-map read, and then **re-scores the portfolio board's alignment column** (`project-lifecycle.md`, the board) against the refreshed theme register. That re-score is the watch's main output interface; the pipeline decides what to do about it.

## The re-aim ladder — acting on the watch, cheapest rung first

1. **Reframe** — reposition existing work's narrative into the current direction's vocabulary: readouts, demos, review evidence. Often captures most of the alignment value at near-zero cost; framing is the cheapest pivot there is.
2. **Reshape** — adjust an active project's scope or emphasis toward the rising theme, through the lifecycle's shaping machinery.
3. **Re-bet** — an actual portfolio pivot: land, hand off, or kill the misaligned project explicitly (stage 8 — never a silent abandonment), then accept or initiate on the rising theme.
4. **Pre-position** — the moves available before any project exists: learn the rising theme's skills early, and invest in relationships aimed by the leader register's trajectory — the people who own rising themes, in the org or beyond it, while those themes are still small. Early relationships on a rising theme are the cheapest ever bought; by the time the theme is established, its people are oversubscribed.

Rungs 2–3 require the ladder's top grades (resourcing, sustained repetition). Rungs 1 and 4 are cheap enough to run on rising-but-unconfirmed signals — which is exactly where they pay best. The reward map acts through a fifth, continuous channel: it tunes everyday working behavior (what to be seen doing, what to never be caught doing) independent of any project move. Standing calibration is inherited from the lifecycle module: early tenure runs the watch at full fidelity but acts mainly on rungs 1 and 4; the right to re-bet grows with delivered standing.

## Trajectory over snapshot

The registers' lifecycle and trajectory states make *slope* visible, and slope is where the career value concentrates — for themes and for people. Established themes are the safe frame for assigned work (rung 1). Emerging and rising themes with accelerating evidence are where initiative bets (lifecycle stage 5) and pre-positioning pay. Rising people are where relationship investment pays: being early on a direction — or early with the person who will own it — is worth more than conforming to a stated one, because by the time a theme or its owner is established, the credit and the access are largely spoken for.

The signal and theme registers ride the store service (`store.md`); capture and refresh sweeps query through the search bus (`search.md`).
